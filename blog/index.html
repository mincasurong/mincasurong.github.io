Project STAMP: Decoupling Multimodal VLA Reasoning from Robot Execution via Deterministic Pre-Dispatch Gating and Fault Write-Back

Allowing a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) planner to dispatch unverified trajectories directly to robot controllers risks kinematic singularities, table collisions, and multi-arm zone contention. Project STAMP introduces a 4-stage closed-loop architecture (Plan → Gate → Execute → Write-Back) that intercepts 100% of physical faults across 7-DoF inverse kinematics, 3D AABB collision volumes, joint/torque limits, and temporal zone locks, converting rejected DAG nodes into structured geometric repair hints.

Core Systems Result: Across 1,000 multi-arm episodes with 60% synthetic fault injection (STAMP-Bench-V1), STAMP intercepts 610/610 hardware faults (0 unverified dispatches) and raises task completion from 39.0% (single-shot VLA) to 100.0% via 2-turn closed-loop write_limit_back recovery.

Building a Multi-Agent Coding Harness from Scratch and Why It Failed: Inside m9g-agent-corps vs. OmO, OpenCode, Claude Code, and Antigravity

We engineered a 28-module, 169-test multi-agent coding harness from scratch (m9g-agent-corps), investigating immutable prefix caching (95% discount), SHA-256 hashline drift-tolerant editing, AST structural skeletonization (75–90% compression), shadow GIT_INDEX_FILE checkpointing, and a 5-tier self-healing hook pipeline. Here is why our standalone harness still failed against OmO, OpenCode, Claude Code, and Antigravity—and how we pivoted its substrates into the m9g-mcp power-tool server.

Core Systems Result: Isolated subsystems cannot compensate for a loosely coupled inner loop: unwired verification gates, false-positive prose-regex handoffs, 300s blocking CLI pipes, mandatory delegation hops, and 12k middle-out string slicing caused our standalone orchestrator to lag pre-existing harnesses until we extracted its 7 deterministic tools over MCP.

Symbolic 3D Grounding and Software-Pipelined VLA Task Planning for Multi-Arm Manipulation

We investigate whether multi-manipulator task planning requires continuous overhead RGB camera streams when real-time 3D coordinate frames (/tf) and deterministic kinematic guardrails are available. Across a 9-block shared-workspace tower assembly task on three Franka Emika FR3 arms, pairing a single Turn-0 Gemini 4 Argon strategic planner with a zero-thinking-budget Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite step executor discovers an optimal 10-turn software-pipelined schedule—reducing API cost by 76.6% ($0.01972 vs. $0.08437) and early-turn latency by 68.7%.

Core Systems Result: Interleaving outer-table picks with central-tower placements eliminates the wave-locked barriers observed in Gemini Robotics-ER 2 (10 turns vs. 13 turns), avoiding 10,929 quadratic history tokens at the tail of the episode while achieving 20/20 safety verifier compliance.
