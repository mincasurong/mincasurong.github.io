Why Browser-Native 3D Multi-Robot TAMP Is Difficult: How We Built the Zero-Install Workcell & 6-Cell Smart Factory Lite-Sim at tamp.mincasurong.com

Evaluating multi-manipulator Task and Motion Planning (TAMP), pre-dispatch safety gating, and factory-scale fleet orchestration traditionally requires a 24 GB VRAM workstation running NVIDIA Isaac Sim or a ROS 2 / MoveIt 2 container cluster. To make our Lazy-DaSH and Project STAMP architectures immediately verifiable in any browser, we engineered and deployed tamp.mincasurong.com (fast_sim_web), a zero-install 60 FPS Three.js WebGL simulator uniting an 8-slot Franka FR3 cooperative workcell and an 18-arm + 4-AMR Smart Factory digital twin. Here is a transparent breakdown of the five core engineering bottlenecks that make browser-native 3D TAMP difficult, and the exact mathematical and spatial solutions we built to overcome them.

1. Hurdle 1: Heavy GPU Simulator Boot & the 24 GB VRAM Barrier vs. 134 KB Binary STL Pack at 60 FPS

The first obstacle to interactive multi-robot TAMP research on the web is the sheer weight of modern physics and rendering stacks. Our full workstation pipeline in IsaacSim_Gemini relies on NVIDIA Isaac Sim 6.0 (Omniverse Kit + PhysX 5 GPU tensors) or a WSL2 ROS 2 Jazzy + MoveIt 2 + MuJoCo 3.x container stack. While indispensable for photorealistic synthetic data generation and 240 Hz contact physics, that stack demands an RTX 3090/4090 class GPU with 16–24 GB of VRAM, 45–90 seconds of cold simulator boot time, and gigabytes of USD asset downloads - making it impossible for a reviewer or robotics engineer to test our algorithms on a standard laptop or tablet.

Alternative cloud-streaming approaches (such as Omniverse WebRTC pixel streaming) shift the bottleneck from the client GPU to expensive cloud GPU instances that suffer from network latency, session queues, and input lag during interactive camera orbiting or slot reconfiguration. To make https://tamp.mincasurong.com instantaneously accessible to anyone, we engineered fast_sim_web to run 100% client-side in standard WebGL at a locked 60 FPS:

  • Decimated Binary STL Asset Pack (134 KB Total): Raw CAD and URDF visual meshes for the 7-DoF Franka Emika FR3 manipulator and parallel-jaw hand routinely exceed 18–35 MB per robot. We decimated and binary-packed the nine canonical rigid bodies - link0.stl through link7.stl plus hand.stl - down to a combined 134 KB binary payload without losing silhouette accuracy around joint collars, wrist flanges, or finger pads.
  • Single-Fetch Geometry Caching & Scene Cloning: Whether the user spawns 2 arms in the workcell or 22 total FR3 manipulators (18 stationary cell arms + 4 AMR-mounted arms) in the Smart Factory mode, the browser fetches the 134 KB STL buffer only once on initial page load and clones the underlying Three.js BufferGeometry instances across every kinematic chain.
  • Deterministic 60 FPS Render Loop: By decoupling high-rate kinematic interpolation and collision checking from shadow-map and material allocation, tamp.mincasurong.com achieves a sub-400 ms cold boot and maintains 60 FPS interactive orbit/pan/zoom inspection across desktop and mobile browsers.

2. Hurdle 2: Single-Threaded Browser Kinematics & Collision Verification (7-DoF Modified DH, 6×7 DLS IK & 128 Hz Swept Capsule Gate)

The second major hurdle is mathematical: browsers execute JavaScript/TypeScript on a single UI thread without native C++ MoveIt 2, KDL, or CUDA cuRobo bindings. If an Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver or swept-volume collision checker blocks the main thread for even 100 ms - or allocates excessive temporary matrices that trigger garbage-collection pauses - the 60 FPS WebGL viewport stutters and interactive slot toggling feels broken. Worse, simplified 3-DoF analytic arm approximations fail to capture true 7-DoF elbow redundancy, wrist singularities, or joint-limit saturations on the Franka FR3.

We solved this in tamp.mincasurong.com by writing a zero-dependency, allocation-conscious TypeScript kinematic and safety verification engine that executes in microseconds per step:

Browser-Native Franka FR3 7-DoF Kinematic & Safety Gate Specification Ref: M9G-TR-2026-06 • fast_sim_web/src/kinematics.ts
1. 7-DoF Modified Denavit-Hartenberg Forward Kinematics: Chains exact Craig Modified DH link transforms (d1 = 0.333 m, d3 = 0.316 m, d5 = 0.384 m, a4 = +0.0825 m, a5 = -0.0825 m, a7 = 0.088 m, flange offset 0.107 m) to compute all 7 joint frame poses and the end-effector tool center point (TCP).
2. Analytical 6×7 Geometric Jacobian: Extracts the 3D rotation axis and origin of every revolute joint directly from the forward kinematic chain to populate the 3×7 linear velocity rows (cross product of joint axis and lever arm to TCP) and 3×7 angular velocity rows.
3. Gaussian Elimination Damped Least-Squares (DLS) IK: Solves the 6×6 damped normal system with damping factor lambda = 0.02 plus a null-space posture attraction term (weight 0.05 toward the nominal upright home configuration), converging to sub-millimeter position accuracy within 150 iterations in under 25 ms while clamping all 7 joints strictly inside FR3 URDF limits.
4. 128 Hz Swept Capsule Safety Gate: Discretizes every candidate multi-arm trajectory at 128 Hz and evaluates analytical capsule-to-capsule and capsule-to-AABB distances across all active links, emitting deterministic fault codes (IK_UNREACHABLE, WORKSPACE_COLLISION, BIMANUAL_PROMOTION_REQUIRED) and rendering translucent red 3D collision-ghost meshes at the exact fault configuration.
  • Why Null-Space Posture Regularization Matters in the Browser: Without the 0.05 null-space bias toward the canonical elbow-up home posture, pure minimum-norm 7-DoF IK solutions drift into awkward elbow-down configurations after sequential pick-and-place cycles, causing spurious link-to-table collisions.
  • Interactive 3D Red Collision-Ghost Inspection: Whenever the 128 Hz Swept Capsule Safety Gate intercepts a fault (such as two arms entering a shared central handover zone simultaneously or an arm attempting to reach an out-of-envelope cloth corner), tamp.mincasurong.com spawns a translucent red 3D ghost manipulator frozen at the exact rejected pose alongside the structured fault certificate - making Project STAMP's pre-dispatch gating visually transparent.

3. Hurdle 3: Reconfigurable 8-Slot Workcell (s0..s7) & Sub-60 ms Lazy-DaSH Replanning Across 3 Cooperative Modes

The third bottleneck is combinatorial: in our interactive 8-slot workcell at tamp.mincasurong.com, users can click any combination of 8 peripheral mounting slots (s0..s7) around the worktable, inject workspace obstacles, and switch between three physically distinct manipulation regimes. Recomputing an entire multi-robot task and motion schedule from scratch whenever a slot is toggled or an obstacle is dropped would freeze the UI.

By implementing UIUC Parasol Lab's Lazy-DaSH (Lee et al., IEEE T-RO) task-space hypergraph expansion and branch-scoped conflict repair directly in TypeScript, our workcell planner synthesizes and repairs multi-arm schedules in 27.1 ms to 34.7 ms - well below our 60 ms interactive budget:

4. Hurdle 4: 3D Spatial Clearance & Fleet Scaling in the 6-Cell Smart Factory (18 FR3 Arms + 4 Mobile Manipulators MM0..MM3)

Expanding tamp.mincasurong.com from a single 8-slot worktable to a full 6-Cell Smart Factory digital twin - comprising 18 stationary cell FR3 manipulators (3 per cell across 6 manufacturing cells), 4 autonomous mobile manipulators (MM0..MM3), 3-tier Automated Storage and Retrieval System (AS/RS) racks, and an Automated Roller Shipping Dock - exposed our fourth major engineering hurdle: 3D spatial clearance, Z-fighting, and visual/kinematic fleet congestion.

In early prototypes of the Smart Factory mode, when mobile manipulators MM0..MM3 docked alongside a workcell table to pick or deliver parts, three geometric failures occurred simultaneously: (1) the factory floor and table base pedestals suffered depth-buffer Z-fighting, (2) the AMR chassis visually clipped into the table apron while the AMR-mounted FR3 base sat too low relative to the tabletop, causing Link 1/Link 2 collisions with the table edge during reach-in, and (3) dense warehouse racking and excessive AMR counts obscured the inter-cell aisles. We engineered five precise geometric fixes to achieve a clean, collision-free 720.0 units/hour factory digital twin:

6-Cell Smart Factory Spatial Clearance & Elevation Invariants Ref: M9G-TR-2026-06 • fast_sim_web Smart Factory Mode
1. Floor Plane Depression (Z = -0.52 m): Lowered the global factory floor plane and aisle travel surface to Z = -0.52 m relative to the workcell tabletop datum (Z = 0.00 m), completely eliminating Z-fighting between AMR wheels, cell pedestals, and table legs.
2. Vertical AMR Turret Riser (Z = +0.02 m Base Elevation): Added a rigid vertical turret riser column atop each mobile base (MM0..MM3) so that the mounted Franka FR3 link0 base flange sits at Z = +0.02 m above the tabletop plane, giving the mobile arm identical downward reach-in clearance as stationary table-mounted arms.
3. Elevated Overhead Transfer Pose: Raised the intermediate transit and handover waypoint Z-height during AMR-to-cell and cell-to-shipping-dock transfers so swept forearm and gripper capsules clear tabletop fixtures by a strict vertical safety margin.
4. Strict Table-Grid Clamping: Clamped all cell buffer slots, WIP trays, and assembly target coordinates strictly inside the interior table bounding polygon so no target ever spawns near the bevelled table edge.
5. 50% Warehouse Rack & AMR Density Right-Sizing: Reduced AS/RS rack footprint and active mobile manipulator density by 50% (streamlining to MM0..MM3 and compact 3-tier racks), opening unobstructed aisle sightlines and sustaining 720.0 units/hour throughput (60.0 s makespan across 12 multi-stage orders).

5. Hurdle 5: Multi-Pane Telemetry Dock & Responsive Execution Hypergraph Layout

A TAMP simulator is only as useful as its observability. Beyond the 3D WebGL viewport, researchers inspecting tamp.mincasurong.com need to see the live Real-Time Execution Hypergraph (showing task vertices, active composite transitions, and branch-scoped conflict repairs), the Multi-Arm Spatio-Temporal Gantt Schedule, and the Deterministic Safety Gate Telemetry Log simultaneously.

In standard fixed-grid web dashboards, displaying a 3D canvas alongside a node-edge hypergraph and a 4-to-22 robot Gantt timeline on varying screen aspect ratios causes severe text clipping, overlapping SVG labels, or forced horizontal scrollbars. We solved this UI engineering bottleneck with a two-part responsive layout engine:

  1. Interactive Mouse-Draggable Split Resizers (.resizer-h, .resizer-v-inner, .resizer-v): Every boundary between the 3D WebGL canvas, the control sidebar, the Execution Hypergraph pane, and the bottom Gantt/telemetry dock is governed by pointer-captured drag resizers with clamped minimum/maximum flex percentages. Users can smoothly drag .resizer-h and .resizer-v-inner to enlarge the 3D factory view or expand the hypergraph inspection pane on demand.
  2. ResizeObserver-Driven Dynamic Font & Node Scaling: We attached a dedicated browser ResizeObserver to the Execution Hypergraph and Gantt containers that continuously measures the live pixel width and height of each sub-pane and dynamically scales node radii, stroke widths, and typography (clamp() + container-proportional font sizing). As a result, hypergraph state labels and multi-arm timeline bars never clip, wrap awkwardly, or collide - even when resizing panes during a live 60 FPS simulation run.
